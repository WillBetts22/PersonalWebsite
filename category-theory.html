Research

Compositional Inconsistency as a Verification Signal: Pilot Results on MuSiQue

Author: William Betts
Date: November 11, 2026
Type: Pilot results paper

Abstract: Compositional inconsistency is the case where a model answers a multi-hop question differently in one step than step by step. It predicts errors on short chains and in weaker models, but as pre-specified it does not add predictive value beyond confidence and self-consistency at scale. We sampled both routes on MuSiQue chain questions with three OpenAI models (gpt-4.1, gpt-4.1-mini, gpt-5.5), labelled every claim against gold answers, and scored selection strategies under the parent project's fixed verification budget (25% of claims checked, swept from 6% to 63%). On 100 two-hop questions, composition was the best non-oracle strategy for all three models, and for gpt-4.1-mini it raised error-prediction AUROC by +0.139 (95% CI +0.035 to +0.250). On 300 questions with 2-, 3- and 4-hop chains, the pre-specified score (maximum over chain regions) showed no gain for either gpt-4.1-mini (+0.001) or gpt-5.5 (−0.007). An exploratory diagnosis traces much of the gpt-4.1-mini null to that aggregator: the full-chain score recovers a +0.053 gain on 3–4 hop chains (CI +0.006 to +0.102). Stated confidence was near chance in every open-book run (AUROC 0.48–0.60). These are pilot results on MuSiQue train; the full-chain hypothesis needs pre-registration and a held-out test.

Detecting Hallucinations Through Compositional Inconsistency: A Categorical Approach to Budgeted Verification

Type: Research proposal

Research telos: Language models can recall individual facts correctly and still fail to combine them into a correct answer. The aim of this project is to use category theory to say precisely when two ways of computing an answer should agree, and to test whether failures of that agreement can flag hallucinations and tell us which claims are most worth verifying on a fixed budget.

Abstract: Large language models can correctly recall individual facts while failing to combine them into a correct answer. This project investigates whether failures of compositional consistency provide a useful signal for detecting factual hallucinations and selecting claims for verification. We represent a multi-step task as a graph of typed operations and use category theory to specify when different computational paths should produce equivalent answers. We then measure disagreement between a model's direct execution of a composite task and its execution of the constituent steps, without accessing ground-truth answers during detection.

When the Pieces Agree: A Gentle Introduction to Operadic Consistency in AI Reasoning

Author: William Betts
Date: October 20, 2026

An attempt to rewrite recent work on operads and compositional reasoning in LLMs so that it is more accessible to people without a background in category theory. It builds up from functions and composition to categories, functors, operads, and their algebras, using simple arithmetic examples.
